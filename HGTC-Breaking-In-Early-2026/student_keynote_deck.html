// HARD TRUTHS

The "College Trap" Most Students Fall Into

The greatest illusion is believing that graduating with a degree guarantees a career.

❌ The Default Passive Path

1. Only complete assigned coursework & homework.

2. Keep a 3.8 GPA but possess 0 public code or lab projects.

3. Wait until Senior Spring semester to send 500 LinkedIn resumes.

4. Meet zero working engineers outside of professors.

Result: Filtered out by ATS bots and competing with 10,000 identical grads.
✅ The High-Velocity Active Path

1. Build a $150 used mini-PC homelab running Linux & Proxmox.

2. Commit real code or vulnerability write-ups to GitHub weekly.

3. Show up at local meetups (CCC, BSides, Hackathons) as a freshman/sophomore.

4. Work on open-source, capture CTF flags, and connect with directors directly.

Result: Hired through backchannel referral before even submitting a generic resume.
// SOCIAL LEVERAGE

Why You Must Join Networks ASAP

Senior tech leaders love mentoring hungry students. But they can't help people they don't know exist.

🤝 The Student Superpower

When you say "I'm a student fascinated by what you build, can I ask you 2 questions?", response rates are 80%+. Once you graduate, you become another cold salesperson.

🚪 The Hidden Job Market

Over 75% of top tech and security roles are filled before ever hitting LinkedIn. They are filled by people who said: "Hey, I know a kid from the local meetup who built something cool."

📈 Accelerated Signal

When you talk to professionals weekly, you discover what technologies companies actually run (Linux, Docker, Terraform, Splunk) vs what textbooks taught from 2014.

Rule of Thumb: Every tech event or Discord you join expands your opportunity surface area exponentially.
// TACTICAL ADVICE

How to Be Active (Without Being Annoying)

Networking isn't about collecting business cards; it's about demonstrating curiosity and proof-of-work.

🛠️ 1. Show Your Work

Don't just say you like cybersecurity. Publish a 2-page writeup on how you set up Wireshark, cracked your first CTF challenge, or deployed a Pi-hole on your dorm WiFi.

🙋 2. Ask Surgical Questions

Avoid: "How do I get a job?"
Ask instead: "I built a mini SIEM in Docker, but my syslog buffer is dropping packets. How do you handle high volume ingest in prod?"

🙌 3. Volunteer at Events

Volunteer to run the mic, manage badges, or set up cables at local conferences (Hack Myrtle, BSides). You get full free access and direct 1-on-1 time with keynote speakers.
