# Speaker Notes & Talk Outline: Breaking In Early

**Audience:** College / University students (Computer Science, Cybersecurity, IT, Engineering)  
**Host / Venue:** Conway Computer Club / Coastal Carolina / Tech Meetups  
**Slide File:** [student_keynote_deck.html](file:///f:/OneDrive/ConwayComputerClub/slides/student_keynote_deck.html)

---

## Slide 1: Breaking In Early (Title & Hook)
- **Goal:** Dispel the myth that tech careers start on graduation day.
- **Talking Points:**
  - "Most students wait until their final semester to start thinking about jobs. By then, it's too late to build momentum."
  - Introduce the focus: Real day-to-day work, breaking into offensive security, and the hidden networking advantage students have right now.

---

## Slide 2: Who Am I & What Do I Do?
- **Goal:** Establish credibility through real industry practice and community leadership.
- **Talking Points:**
  - Day job: Director of Offensive Security Research (red teaming, adversary simulation, AI vulnerability research).
  - Community: Founder of Conway Computer Club, Hack Myrtle, Mentor Village.
  - Core philosophy: **Don't learn to hack — hack to learn.** 
    - The best engineers and security researchers don't memorize attack scripts; they dive in, break things, fix them, and discover how the systems truly function under the hood.
    - Going through the struggle of breaking a system, diagnosing why it failed, and fixing it builds 10x deeper intuition than passive textbook reading. That grit and curiosity goes a long way.

---

## Slide 3: Day in the Life: Part 1 (Morning Sprints)
- **Goal:** Demystify what an offensive security engineer / tech lead actually does in the first half of the day.
- **Talking Points:**
  - **8:00 - 9:30 AM:** CVE and intelligence triage. Threat actors don't sleep; waking up and scanning what's new.
  - **9:30 - 11:30 AM:** Deep technical execution. Hands-on coding, reverse engineering, containerized attack simulations.
  - **11:30 - 12:30 PM:** Bridging the gap with defensive teams. In the real world, being an offensive engineer isn't just about breaking things—it's about helping engineers build resilient architectures.

---

## Slide 4: Day in the Life: Part 2 (Afternoon Execution & Community)
- **Goal:** Highlight automation, executive communication, and giving back.
- **Talking Points:**
  - **1:30 - 3:30 PM:** Automation & tooling. Nobody runs manual tests all day; we write Python, Go, and AI agent workflows to do the heavy lifting.
  - **3:30 - 5:00 PM:** Executive debriefs. If you can't explain an exploit to a non-technical VP, the bug doesn't get fixed. Writing and communication are super-skills.
  - **Evenings:** CCC, Hack Myrtle, Proxmox homelab tinkering.

---

## Slide 5: The "College Trap" Most Students Fall Into
- **Goal:** Shake up passive mindsets without disparaging their education.
- **Talking Points:**
  - Contrast **The Default Passive Path** (3.8 GPA, zero external code, sending 500 LinkedIn resumes into the black hole) with **The Active Builder Path** (homelabs, public GitHub repos, community presence).
  - Emphasize: Employers in tech care far more about *proof-of-work* than course transcripts.

---

## Slide 6: The Superpower of Joining Networks ASAP
- **Goal:** Teach the unique leverage college students have.
- **Talking Points:**
  - **The Student Superpower:** When you reach out as an eager student asking for 10 minutes of advice, senior engineers love to help. Once you graduate, you look like another applicant. Use the student card aggressively now!
  - **The Hidden Job Market:** 75%+ of the best roles are filled through referrals and community familiarity.

---

## Slide 7: How to Be Active (Without Being Annoying)
- **Goal:** Practical etiquette for networking without feeling transactional.
- **Talking Points:**
  - **Show Your Work:** Don't just claim interest; write short summaries of homelab experiments or CTF writeups.
  - **Ask Surgical Questions:** Don't ask "How do I get an internship?" Ask specific questions about tools, architectures, and real-world trade-offs.
  - **Volunteer at Events:** Offer to handle registration desks, AV, or badges at tech conferences like Hack Myrtle and BSides. It puts you in direct contact with every speaker.

---

## Slide 8: Your 4-Pillar Semester Playbook
- **Goal:** Give an actionable 4-step framework they can remember.
  1. **Lab:** Run Linux, set up Proxmox/VirtualBox, test Docker.
  2. **Code:** Automate small repetitive tasks in Python/Bash; push to GitHub.
  3. **Write:** Document what you broke and how you fixed it.
  4. **Connect:** Show up to local meetups (CCC, local user groups, Hackathons).

---

## Slide 9: Action Plan & Next Steps
- **Goal:** Direct call-to-action for the upcoming week.
- **Talking Points:**
  - Clean up your GitHub.
  - Join the Conway Computer Club Discord and get plugged into upcoming build nights.
  - Start playing with hands-on labs (TryHackMe, HackTheBox, local VMs).
  - End on a high note: "Nobody asks for your GPA when you show them a working system you built. Start building today."
